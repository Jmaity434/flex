# Tone reference

Seven tones. Each changes scripting energy, pacing, typography personality, and transition style.

## `default`
**When to use:** Most consumer apps, playful products, general launches.
**Energy:** Playful, clean, postable. The product gets to be funny on its own terms.
**Voice:** First person plural. Warm. Direct. No corporate language.
**Sample storyboard shape:** Hook joke → product reveal → 2 feature beats → warm outro.

## `polished`
**When to use:** Premium tools, design-focused products, serious B2B that still has taste.
**Energy:** Serious, elegant. Restraint is the creative choice.
**Voice:** Third person or no voice. The product speaks for itself.
**Sample storyboard shape:** Quiet open → one strong visual → 2 held feature cards → calm end card.

## `yc-parody`
**When to use:** Absurd products, meme startups, anything that benefits from deadpan seriousness.
**Energy:** Deadpan startup launch energy. The joke is how seriously it's delivered.
**Voice:** Serious. Matter-of-fact. No winking.
**Sample storyboard shape:** Straight-faced claim → product shot → another straight claim → logo like a real Series A video.

## `chaotic`
**When to use:** Loud launches, meme energy, Gen-Z native products.
**Energy:** Fast, loud, aggressive. Over-the-top.
**Voice:** ALL CAPS energy when it helps. Rapid cuts.
**Sample storyboard shape:** 6–8 micro scenes, some under 2s, flash zooms, big type.

## `deadpan`
**When to use:** Dry humor, understated confidence, products that are funny by being quiet.
**Energy:** Calm, dry, understated. The joke is that nothing is a joke.
**Voice:** Minimal. Long holds. Quiet confidence.
**Sample storyboard shape:** 3–4 scenes, lots of empty space, slow fades.

## `cinematic`
**When to use:** Trailer-scale moments, dramatic reveals, high-stakes positioning.
**Energy:** Dramatic, trailer-scale. Big motion, bigger claims.
**Voice:** Epic but still specific to the product.
**Sample storyboard shape:** Dark open → rising claim → product hero → punchy end title.

## `app-store`
**When to use:** Feature-led launches, clean benefit lists, store-style creative.
**Energy:** Smooth, feature-card clean. Corporate but not boring.
**Voice:** Clear benefit statements. Clean slides.
**Sample storyboard shape:** 4–6 feature cards with smooth slides and short labels.

Presets are defaults, not limits. Freeform direction always wins when the user gives it.

For A/B testing (`--ab`), pick two tones that contrast (e.g. `default` vs `yc-parody`, or `polished` vs `chaotic`) so the comparison is useful.
